## Why

An offline measurement of the four-film ignition programme (cross-modal decodability, 2026-10-31) found two defects in the playlist perception feed. Both change what an entity watching the programme actually sees and hears.

1. **The picture runs ahead of the sound on variable-frame-rate films.** `PlaylistSource.read()` picks the frame to show as `int(offset * item.fps)`, the manifest's nominal 25 fps, and treats the frame index as time. First Spaceship on Venus and Gulliver's Travels are variable-frame-rate WebM files that average 23.98 fps (112,984 frames over 4,711.4 s; 109,990 over 4,586.6 s). The picture leads the sound by 25 s at 10 minutes, 153 s at an hour, and 192 s and 187 s when the video runs out about 190 s before the clock and the sound do; it then holds its last frame. The measurement showed this drift erases the time-locked audiovisual structure the programme carries.
2. **The playlist audio is padded.** `PlaylistAudioStream` appends `bytes(rframe.planes[0])` for every resampled frame. A PyAV plane is a padded buffer, larger than the frame's samples (a 193-sample mono s16 frame is a 512-byte plane). The padding is mostly zeros with some stale samples. Decoded that way, every programme film is stretched with gaps: Baby Huey 539 s for a 414 s film, First Spaceship 6,600 s for 4,712 s, Gulliver 5,831 s for 4,587 s, Santa Claus 7,467 s for 4,852 s. Because the audio is paced at 1x, the entity hears each film broken up by the inserted padding, the sound falls further behind the picture every minute, and it is still hearing one film when the clock has moved on to the next.

Related assumptions in the same feed: the video registers each item's length on the shared clock as `CAP_PROP_FRAME_COUNT / item.fps`, which is only right when the nominal rate is the real one; the audio producer re-anchors its pacing after a late block, so every stall adds a lag that never goes away; it moves to the next item as soon as an item's audio track ends (a missing or short track puts the sound on the next film early); and the ignition analysis measures picture-to-sound drift between the programme offset and the audio feed's offset even when the two are on different items, which turns a 30 ms boundary skew into a drift of a whole film.

## What Changes

- **Video by presentation timestamp.** The playlist video source decodes each item with PyAV and presents, at each read, the latest frame whose presentation time (`pts × time_base`, relative to the container start) is at or before the shared clock's offset into the item. The manifest `fps` is no longer used for timing; it stays in the manifest as recorded provenance. Missing timestamps are filled from the previous frame plus the stream's frame interval, and duplicate or backward timestamps are held at the previous time, so presentation time never runs backwards. A source opened or re-opened partway into an item seeks to the nearest keyframe at or before the clock's offset and decodes forward, instead of decoding from the first frame. Frames are converted to BGR only when presented.
- **One item length for both surfaces.** Both surfaces register an item's length on the shared clock from the container duration (`container.duration` in AV_TIME_BASE units). When a container reports no duration, the video registers the end of its last frame when it reaches the end of the stream.
- **Sample-exact audio.** The playlist audio takes exactly `frame.samples` samples per channel from each resampled frame (interleaved for planar layouts), flushes the resampler at the end of each item, and places the decoded audio on the item's timeline by the decoded frames' timestamps: a gap in the audio timestamps longer than 40 ms is filled with silence, and audio that starts more than 40 ms before the current position (after a seek, or overlapping timestamps) is trimmed. At the end of an item the audio is padded with silence, or trimmed, to the item's length on the shared clock, so each item's sound starts when the clock reaches it. An item with no audio track is heard as silence for its length instead of being skipped.
- **Audio paced against the shared clock.** Each audio block goes out when the shared clock reaches its place on the item timeline. After a stall the owed blocks are emitted back to back, up to 0.5 s owed; beyond that the producer skips forward to the clock's position and logs it once, without content. The producer used to pace itself on its own deadlines and re-anchor after a late block, so every stall added a lag that never went away. The video source picks its frame from the clock on every read and has no such lag.
- **Drift on the same item only.** The ignition analysis measures picture-to-sound drift only for records whose audio position is on the programme's item. Records where the audio is on another item are counted in the data-quality block as `audio_on_other_item`.
- **Dependency.** `av` (PyAV, already pinned by the `[audio]` extra) joins the `[vision]` extra, since playlist video now decodes with it. OpenCV stays for the live camera.
