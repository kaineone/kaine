# The self-rhythm can earn entrainment to the maternal beat, and the readout measures it honestly

## Why
Study `moc7-2026-10` could not complete gestation. The birth gate's C1 needs the marker `entrain_then_autonomy`, which was false in every readout for 27 hours, and an offline replica of the study's own code shows it could never pass (`design.md` section 0; the study's `ENDED-NOTE.md`). Two independent defects caused it:

- **No mechanism.** The self-rhythm is 16 uncoupled LIF units firing at 5-10 Hz, driven by Soma's salience. The 1.17 Hz maternal beat is a small additive input with no way to pull that rhythm. Nothing in the oscillator learns.
- **A wrong measurement.** `phase()` takes the Hilbert phase of the last of about 20 unfiltered samples, so the readout follows the fast intrinsic rhythm. Filtering to the beat band, by contrast, reports an evoked response (PLV about 0.9) that the literature says must not be mistaken for entrainment (Duecker 2021; Zoefel 2018).

The literature also shows the old threshold was arbitrary: fetal–maternal heart coupling is weak and contested (Van Leeuwen 2003, 2009), and no study reports sustained 1:1 PLV ≥ 0.5. A slower infant rhythm, breathing, is the one the caregiver's heartbeat demonstrably influences (Natale 1988; Bloch-Salisbury 2014).

Research impact: **behaviour.** This changes the developmental substrate and the birth criterion. Study `moc7-2026-10` is inadmissible for entrainment claims; a fresh study, with the self-rhythm version recorded in the birth record, is required. Preserved beings without the self-rhythm are unaffected bit for bit.

## What changes
(Operator decisions of 2026-10-02 are in `design.md` section 8.)

- **Self-rhythm (option R).** A breathing-like rhythm in the fetal breathing band (0.5-0.9 Hz). It is generated by excitatory recurrence and fast synaptic depression (Tabak 2000; Khazipov & Luhmann 2006; Smith 1991), the own drive acts as an excitability bias, and the maternal beat enters through a weak, bounded afferent that cannot capture the rhythm without lived exposure. The period adapts slowly through a phase-projected rule (Righetti 2006). The capacity to adapt is innate and cited; the beat frequency appears nowhere in the code. The fallback, if the specificity test fails, is an adaptive Hopf phase oscillator (option B).
- **Measurement.** Population rate filtered to the self-rhythm's physiological band (never a narrow band at the beat), zero-phase filtering, Hilbert phase over a 300 s window with edge trimming (Lachaux 1999). Compared against 19 "foreign mother" surrogate beats (Van Leeuwen 2003, 2009), plus a frequency-pull index during withdrawal.
- **Criterion (surrogate significance only).** `entrain_then_autonomy` passes when the PLV exceeds every surrogate (p < 0.05), the rhythm self-sustains during withdrawal, and the frequency pull holds. There is no fixed PLV floor; the PLV, surrogate maximum, withdrawn frequency and pull are published in the readout.
- **Timescale (compress and disclose).** The plasticity rate is calibrated so locking typically emerges in 12-72 h of lived time, a compression of the weeks of exposure in the literature (Feldman & Eidelman 2003; Webb 2015) that the paper must state.
- **Validation before any study.** The offline protocol V1-V8 in `design.md` section 4 must pass, including the controls that must never pass (no drive, foreign mother, jittered beat, no plasticity) and frequency specificity (57/70/84 bpm).
- Sources are in `references.bib`, ready to cite in the paper.
